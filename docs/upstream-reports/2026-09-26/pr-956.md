# PR #956: Alternative method to get news data

Upstream: TauricResearch/TradingAgents#956 by kahonn1002, head `21277d6738ff`, opened 2026-06-03,
last activity 2026-06-30. +291 / -17 across 4 files.

## Summary

**Verdict: adapt.** Google News RSS gives far better non-US and ETF news coverage than yfinance
for this user's watchlist, and it can be queried point in time with the `after:`/`before:`
operators. The PR uses neither the operators nor a sane default, so Berkshire should take the
source and build it into `tool_news` itself, with its existing window, gap and failure rules.

## What it does

- It adds `tradingagents/dataflows/google_news.py`, a keyless news vendor. It asks the Google News RSS search
  endpoint about several query variants (company name from yfinance `shortName`, stock code,
  "Malaysia stock"), removes duplicate titles, and filters by `pubDate` on the client
  (`google_news.py:111-191`). The global news function runs a fixed list of queries, three of
  them about Malaysia or ASEAN (`google_news.py:227-232`).
- It registers the vendor for `get_news`/`get_global_news` and makes it the **global default**
  (`default_config.py`: `news_data: "google_news"`). It adds a `.KL` benchmark (`^KLSE`) and four
  Malaysia queries to `global_news_queries`.
- The problem it addresses: yfinance and Alpha Vantage return little or no news for tickers
  outside the US or large exchanges (the author trades Bursa Malaysia).

## Soundness

Point-in-time behaviour is the weak spot, and it matters most here.

- **It never scopes the query to the window.** `_fetch_rss` (`google_news.py:54-108`) sends only
  `q`/`hl`/`gl`/`ceid`, so Google returns up to 100 items ranked by recency and relevance *as of
  today*, and the code then filters them on the client. On a past date the window usually falls
  outside what came back, and the tool reports `"No news found for {ticker} between ..."`
  (`google_news.py:189`). That is exactly the "absence vs unavailable" confusion Berkshire's
  REQ-DATA-05 forbids. The fix was available. I probed the endpoint on 2026-09-26:
  `q="Rheinmetall after:2025-06-01 before:2025-06-08"` returned 18 items, all dated 2025-06-02 to
  06-07, and `after:2026-03-01 before:2026-03-09` returned 40 items inside the window. So Google
  News RSS *is* windowable. The PR just doesn't use it.
- **Look-ahead leaks:**
  - Undated items skip the date filter entirely (`google_news.py:178`, `if start_dt and article["pub_date"]`).
    A date string that fails to parse sets `start_dt = end_dt = None` and turns filtering off.
  - The global news function has no lower bound. It checks only `> curr_dt + 1 day` (`google_news.py:252`),
    and it cuts to `[:limit]` *before* that check (`google_news.py:251`), so future items use up the
    limit and older items sit under a header that claims a 7-day window.
  - The end bound `<= end_date + 1 day` (`google_news.py:179`) includes midnight of the next day.
- **Timezone crash (latent):** the `%z` fallback in `_parse_rss_date` (`google_news.py:46`) gives
  aware datetimes, which are then compared with naive bounds at lines 179/252 and raise a `TypeError`. Today's
  feed sends `GMT` (it parses as naive, so no crash), which is why it hasn't fired.
- **Scope regressions:** the default switch sends every US user to the Malaysian edition
  (`google_news.py:58-60` hard-codes `hl=en-MY, gl=MY, ceid=MY:en`). The diff also deletes the
  `.SS`/`.SZ` benchmarks from `benchmark_map`, which silently turns the alpha benchmark for A-shares
  into SPY.
- **Thin output:** the feed gives headlines only. The RSS `<description>` is an anchor tag repeating the title, and the
  `link` is a `news.google.com/rss/articles/...` redirect. The PR also drops the per-article date from the
  output, so the analyst cannot judge how recent an item is.
- **Failure behaviour:** network and parse errors return `[]`, which then becomes "No news found".
  Again, a failure is reported as an absence.
- **Caveats that remain even with a correct implementation:** historical items carry rounded
  times (07:00/08:00 GMT), so dates are only accurate to the day. The index and headline text are Google's
  *current* versions (edited titles, removed articles). The endpoint is unofficial and has no SLA.

## Value to Berkshire

The value is real and specific to this user.

- **Coverage gap, measured 2026-09-26** with Berkshire's own `_news_item` over `yf.Ticker(..).news`:

  | Ticker | yfinance news | Google News RSS (en-US) |
  |---|---|---|
  | RHM.DE | 10 items spread over 2026-08-10..09-25, so about 1–2 per week | 100 items (`Rheinmetall stock news`), and 99 in the last 7 days with `when:7d` |
  | LHA.DE | 10 items over 2026-08-05..09-20 | 77 items |
  | EIMI.L | **0 items** | 24 items |
  | AAPL (US control) | 10 items over 2 days | not needed |

  For RHM.DE the 7-day window that `tool_news` sees holds a handful of items. For EIMI.L it holds
  nothing, and Berkshire correctly flags that as unavailable (`berkshire/data.py:580-588`). But
  the News Analyst then falls back on WebSearch, which is not point in time and has no reliable
  dates (REQ-DATA-07 labels it as current).
- **Point-in-time improvement for past dates and backtests.** The yfinance feed only serves recent
  items. For any backtest older than a few weeks, `tool_news` returns only the gap marker. A windowed
  Google News query returns dated headlines from inside the historical window, which is a real
  grounding gain for `/berkshire:backtest` and the reflection loop. Nothing Berkshire has today
  provides this.
- **Berkshire is already ahead of the PR** in handling: window clamping (`as_of_window`, REQ-DATA-01),
  UTC-aware dates, undated items trusted only on a live window (`berkshire/data.py:572-577`), the
  gap marker (REQ-DATA-05), the `DATA_UNAVAILABLE` contract (REQ-DATA-06), and configurable global
  queries (`berkshire/config.py:28-30`). What Berkshire needs from the PR is the source, not the code.
- **Fit:** it causes no conflict with D1–D8. The engine/agent split holds (a deterministic data tool), the models stay Claude-only,
  and it has no effect on execution. Licence: the idea is re-implemented in about 40 lines, and no code is copied.

## Proposed Berkshire change

Adapt the source into the existing `news` tool. Do not add a new vendor switch.

1. `berkshire/data.py`: add `_google_news_items(query, start, end)`. It requests
   `https://news.google.com/rss/search` with `q=f"{query} after:{start-1d} before:{end+1d}"` and
   `hl=en-US&gl=US&ceid=US:en`. It widens the window by one day on each side because the operators work
   at day granularity, then clamps with the existing `in_window`. It parses `pubDate` with
   `email.utils.parsedate_to_datetime` (always aware), takes the publisher from `<source>`, and leaves the summary empty.
2. `tool_news` (`berkshire/data.py:602-605`): merge the yfinance items with Google News items for
   the instrument's name (the identity `longName`/`shortName`, already fetched for REQ-CTX-01) and remove duplicates
   by normalized title. Tag each line with its source and mark Google items as headline-only. Compute the gap
   marker from the yfinance items only, because the Google query is windowed and has no recency horizon. A
   Google failure adds a short "Google News unavailable: <cause>" line and never fails the tool. If both
   sources fail, the result is `DATA_UNAVAILABLE` as today.
3. Leave `tool_global_news` alone. Yahoo's global search is adequate, and the Malaysia query set does not transfer.
4. Requirements:
   - Amend **REQ-DATA-05**: the news tool combines Yahoo Finance news with Google News RSS,
     queried inside the window with date operators and clamped to it.
   - Amend **REQ-ROLE-03**: the News Analyst's ticker news covers both sources.
   - Amend **REQ-DATA-07**: Google News headlines are dated to the day, and their text and index are current, not
     archival, so they are labelled as such.
5. Test: add a `TST-DATA-xx` in `tests/test_data.py` with a stubbed `requests.get` returning a fixed RSS document.
   It asserts that the query carries `after:`/`before:` for the clamped window, that out-of-window and future items are dropped,
   that a `+0200` offset `pubDate` parses without a crash, that titles already in the yfinance feed are removed as duplicates,
   and that a network error yields the "unavailable" line, not "no news".
6. `agents/news-analyst.md` (and `sentiment-analyst.md`, which also reads `news`): one line saying Google items
   are headlines only, and that the analyst should WebFetch the original article when an item matters.

## Effort and risk

- **Effort: M.** The code is small (about 40 lines in `data.py`), but the change also needs the stubbed-RSS
  test, three requirement amendments, test-plan and traceability rows, persona lines, and a live check
  on RHM.DE, LHA.DE and EIMI.L for a live date and a past date.
- **Risks:**
  - The unofficial endpoint can rate-limit, change format, or show an EU consent interstitial.
    Scheduled ticks must fail soft, and step 2's failure handling covers that.
  - Name-based queries can pick up the wrong company when names are ambiguous. Pairing the name with the
    ticker, or with the exchange for short names, limits this.
  - Headline-only items with day-level dates can tempt the analyst to over-read them.
    The labelling in steps 4 and 6 addresses this.

## Open questions

- Should `.DE` tickers query the German edition (`hl=de&gl=DE&ceid=DE:de`)? It returned about 99 items
  in 3 weeks for Rheinmetall, compared with about 100 over 5 months in the English edition, so local coverage is much denser. The catch
  is German headlines in an English report. It would be a small suffix-to-edition map if wanted.
- For ETFs such as EIMI.L, the useful news is usually about the index or asset class, not the fund. Should the query use
  the fund category (from `etf_profile`) rather than the fund name?
- Day-level dates mean an item dated on the trade date may postdate the close. Berkshire's
  `in_window` already includes the whole end date for Yahoo news too. Should past-dated runs exclude
  the trade date itself for sources that are only accurate to the day?
- When the 100-item cap is hit inside one window, results are cut off by an unknown ranking. Should the tool
  note the cap when the query returns exactly 100 items?
